As centralized AI, controlled by a handful of corporations, fosters passive consumption and erodes our ability to think critically, a new form of exclusion has emerged.

Synapse is a tool for cognitive inclusion. For my Master's project, I designed an app and framework that allows a user to connect their external services (like email and notes) to create a private, local knowledge base that they alone control. A Socratic AI then guides them through structured reflection on their own data, helping them discover and articulate their core values. This process culminates in building a transparent, personal AI twin that the user truly owns and controls. Synapse is a validated system, materialised through seven prototypes, designed to reclaim our cognitive agency through an act of inclusive design.

## Opportunity

The fundamental challenge is the unprecedented concentration of power in AI development. As Geoffrey Hinton warned, a few profit-driven companies now control the technology that will define our future, creating a system where our safety and agency are not the priority. This power imbalance creates a dangerous downstream effect: a socioeconomic "thinking divide." By designing for passive engagement, these systems actively erode our cognitive abilities, making deep, critical thought a luxury.

This affects everyone, but my project focuses on students and researchers, who are on the front lines of this crisis. Their core work depends on deep thinking, yet they are increasingly given tools that discourage it. They face agential exclusion from controlling their tools, value exclusion from aligning AI with their ethics, and ultimately, cognitive exclusion from their own intellectual potential. The opportunity is to design a new class of tools for intellectual empowerment.

## Process

My process was a human-centred loop of discovery, co-design, and validation. It began with foundational interviews with my target user group: ten researchers from diverse fields like HCI and Anthropology. These conversations were crucial for understanding their needs.

This led to the co-design phase, where I used low-fidelity methods like paper prototyping and drawing in workshops. This allowed users, who were experts in their fields but not necessarily tech-savvy, to imagine and shape the system's reflective process without being intimidated by technology. I also ran workshops with design professionals at Map Project Office to refine the interaction framework.

Finally, I validated the concept through formal user testing of seven working prototypes with my user group across four countries, and sought feedback from academic experts to ensure the project was rigorous and impactful.

## Outcome

The outcome is a validated system that provides a tangible pathway to reclaiming cognitive sovereignty. A student connects their research notes to Synapse. The app's Socratic AI then begins a dialogue, asking questions like, "I see a recurring theme of 'decentralization' in your notes. What core principle does this connect to for you?" The impact of this is profound; as one user reflected, "I knew my habits, but I had never considered their root cause."

As the user answers, Synapse organizes these reflections into a visual "mind map." This becomes their living "Pocket Guide": a personal database of their core values and mental models. This guide then serves as the foundational prompt for a personal AI twin they can build, own, and control. This directly addresses the challenge of powerlessness by providing a concrete tool for agency.

## Reflection

Involving people fundamentally corrected a flawed assumption I held as a designer. I initially believed that my target users, highly educated researchers, would be tech-savvy and understand the mechanics of AI. Through our early conversations and workshops, I realized this was a misconception. They were experts in their domains but often used digital tools as black boxes.

This realization was pivotal. It forced me to abandon complex technical explanations and instead use simple, accessible methods like paper prototyping and drawing. This low-fidelity approach was key; it empowered users to co-design the system's core reflective experience based on their fundamental human needs for organization and clarity, rather than my assumptions about technology. It improved the project immeasurably by grounding it in genuine user empathy, ensuring the final solution was intuitive and truly empowering.

## Inclusivity

Synapse builds on the principle of inclusive design to the defining challenge of our time: the concentration of power in AI. This imbalance creates new forms of exclusion: the thinking divide (cognitive exclusion), the loss of control (agential exclusion), and the lack of representation (data & value exclusion).

Synapse makes critical thinking accessible. Providing a structured method for Socratic reflection, it directly fights the "thinking divide" where critical thought is becoming a luxury, ensuring this is not reserved for a privileged few.

Synapse establishes data and agential sovereignty. It gives individuals, regardless of technical skill, the power to own their data and build their own AI tools, providing a foundation of agency for everyone.

Synapse champions cognitive diversity. Instead of imposing a monolithic AI worldview, it empowers users to build an AI that reflects their unique personal, cultural, and ethical values, ensuring technology serves human diversity.
